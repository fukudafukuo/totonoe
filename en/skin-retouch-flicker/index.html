How to fix flickering skin retouch in Premiere Pro

If a skin adjustment appears and disappears as a face moves, check whether the selected skin area changes between frames. This guide covers Lumetri Color's HSL Secondary controls and the mask controls in our plugin, Totonoe Skin Retouch.

Flicker can have other causes, including flickering lights in the original footage. Compare with the uncorrected clip first: if the source itself flickers, changing the skin selection alone will not fix it.

1. Check whether the selection changes

In Lumetri Color, open HSL Secondary and enable Color/Gray to inspect the selected color range. Play the clip or step through frames. Look for skin areas or mask edges that appear and disappear, then turn the preview off.

If the selection changes, shifts in light, face angle, highlights or compression noise may be contributing. A similar-colored background or clothing can also be selected. Adobe explains these controls in its HSL Secondary guide.

2. Refine the selection in Lumetri Color

  1. Sample skin with the eyedropper. Use the add eyedropper to include skin in brighter and darker frames, then review what else has been selected.
  2. Adjust the selection and try the Denoise and Blur controls in Refine. These controls affect the selected area; check the result throughout the shot.
  3. Reduce the strength of the adjustment if the edge remains obvious. Split shots with major lighting or framing changes and adjust them separately.
  4. Turn off Color/Gray and watch the exported clip in motion. A still frame alone can hide changes over time.

Widening the selection too much can include hair, clothes or the background. Use the subtract eyedropper and check the preview before settling on a range.

For the complete built-in workflow, see how to retouch skin in Premiere Pro.

3. Check Totonoe's mask and Temporal Stability

Totonoe Skin Retouch estimates skin hue on each frame when Auto Detect is on. Temporal Stability moderates that estimate using the current frame and the two preceding frames. Its default is 50; 0 turns it off. It does not correct flickering lights in the source.

  1. Turn on Show Mask and play the shot. Look for changes in the selected area and check whether similar colors in the background are included.
  2. Try narrowing Hue Range if unwanted colors are selected. Adjust Hue Softness if the boundary is too abrupt.
  3. With Auto Detect on, compare Temporal Stability at 0 and 50 on your own footage. Turn Show Mask off and inspect the result in motion.

In our offline test of two eight-second interview-style clips, the average frame-to-frame change in estimated hue was smaller at 50 than at 0. The visible output difference was not apparent on those clips. Other footage may behave differently; this control is not a guarantee against flicker.
